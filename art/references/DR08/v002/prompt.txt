The asset: "DR08 rotting logs overgrown with fungi and moss", THREE variants of an old fallen log that has been rotting on the forest floor for decades, so soft it looks like it would crumble if touched:
- Variant A: short broken chunk, about 2.2 m long and 0.6 m tall, both ends broken and crumbling, sagging in the middle like a saddle and half sunk into the soil, the top of the middle caved in.
- Variant B: about 3.7 m long and 0.7 m tall. LEFT end: rounded, crumbling, hollow, with a small dark cave opening. RIGHT end: torn apart along the grain, a thick bundle of long splintered wood fibres and strips (warm grey-brown, like torn rope or shredded wood) leaning up and out on ONE side only, not a ring of spikes. The middle section sags and is split open on top.
- Variant C: about 5 m long and 0.8 m tall, long log lying low and partly sunk into the ground along its length, the left end a large round hollow opening, the right end crumbled; two holes rotted through the top.
All three:
- Brown-rot wood: wherever the bark is gone the wood is broken into chunky CUBES by deep cracks along and across the grain. The cubes are RUSTY REDDISH-BROWN, soft, porous and crumbly with visible wood fibres, edges chipped and rounded, sizes uneven (5 to 15 cm), some cubes fallen out leaving dark holes. It must clearly read as rotten WOOD, never as bricks, stone wall or masonry. Crumbled cubes and red-brown wood powder spill onto the ground at the foot of the log.
- Remaining bark: loose grey-brown bark SHINGLES (rough, flaky, with pale lichen), lifting and curling at the edges, broken into separate pieces with gaps. Not smooth horizontal layers, not rock strata.
- Thick layered SHELF / BRACKET FUNGI growing in overlapping tiers out of the sides (pale cream, tan and rusty-brown concentric bands with pale cream rims), plus clusters of small tan-brown mushrooms (not white) and a few tiny pale cyan glowing mushrooms in the shade of the hollow ends.
- WHITE MYCELIUM: thin white threads and fan-shaped white webs spreading over the rotten cubes.
- Thick, lumpy, soft dark olive-green moss cushions over the top, draping over the edges, pale grey-green lichen rosettes, a few ferns and small seedlings growing out of the log, a low mound of dark brown soil along both sides of the foot, fallen leaves.
